1Overview & system architecture

Wayfarer has three parts, one per actor. (1) Capture: a phone-based Capture Coach turns our SOP into live guidance and checks each take before anything heavy runs. (2) Process: a local job server runs the reconstruction pipeline on one GPU PC (or a free Kaggle GPU): blur-aware frame selection, COLMAP Structure-from-Motion, 3D Gaussian Splatting, then our own navigation-grid builder, which levels the model, gives it real-world scale and extracts a walkable occupancy map. (3) Experience: a static web viewer streams the compressed splat and lets anyone roam in first person with true wall collision. The same metric grid drives POIs, search and A* routing for Round 2, and anchors AR navigation in the finale.

2Capture SOP: every rule tied to the failure it prevents

Reconstruction quality is decided at capture time. Our SOP (docs/SOP.md) is written for a non-expert with a phone and states the reason next to each rule. Its numbers come from one source, pipeline/capture_requirements.md, which the Capture Coach and the automatic checks both read.

Two modes: one long take (homes, ≤ ~8 min) or room-by-room clips that start and end in a doorway. Frames from all clips are matched together, so the clips register into one model without manual stitching.

3Reconstruction pipeline: video to photoreal 3D

3.1 Blur-aware frame selection

Video is sampled at 8 fps and thinned to at most 500 frames by keeping the sharpest frame in each window (variance of the Laplacian). A frame is rejected only if it falls below 0.55× the sharpest frame within ±2 s, not below a global median: a plain wall is naturally low-contrast yet sharp, while motion blur is low relative to its neighbours. Photo folders keep every photo.

3.2 Structure-from-Motion

We drive COLMAP directly, not through nerfstudio's wrapper, which turns loop detection off and only reads sparse/0; a multi-room walk could then drift or silently lose rooms. Short captures use exhaustive matching, so every revisit closes a loop. Longer ones use sequential matching (overlap 20, quadratic) plus vocabulary-tree loop closure, which is O(n). Mapping uses COLMAP 4.2's global mapper (GLOMAP), which solves all cameras at once. On our home capture it registered 284 / 292 frames in one model at 0.67 px reprojection error in 6.5 min, against 282 frames split into two models in 17 min with incremental mapping. Cameras the mapper placed implausibly far away (> 10× the median distance) are deleted; otherwise they squash the real poses and fill the room with fog. Matches are cached by a hash of the frame list, so an interrupted run resumes.

3.3 3D Gaussian Splatting

Splats were chosen over meshes and NeRFs because they render photoreal, view-dependent appearance in real time in WebGL, and they hold up on thin and glossy structures (chair legs, plants, tiles) where photogrammetry meshes melt. We train splatfacto (gsplat CUDA kernels), initialised from the SfM points, with settings that fit a multi-room scene in 8 GB: images cached on the CPU as uint8, a per-image bilateral grid that absorbs exposure and white-balance shifts between rooms, scale regularisation against needle splats, and SO3×R3 camera refinement. Interrupted runs resume from the last checkpoint. An MCMC trainer (train_mcmc.py) with a hard Gaussian cap is kept as a memory-safe fallback for very large venues.

3.4 Making the twin complete and clean

  • Floor & ceiling fill (surfaces.py): chest-height footage barely sees these planes. We project real pixels from the frames onto the known planes at 2 cm per texel, occlusion-checked against rendered depth, and use LaMa inpainting only for what no frame saw.
  • People removal (--mask-people): masked pixels are ignored by both COLMAP and training, so other frames supply the real background with no ghosts.
  • AI view repair (--ai-repair, Difix3D+ approach): novel views are rendered, repaired by a one-step diffusion model, and fine-tuned back into the 3D.
  • RGB-D fast path (import_rgbd.py): a finished scan (mesh + metric poses + frames) becomes a twin in about a minute with no GPU; kaggle_train.py trains the photoreal splat on a free Kaggle GPU.

4Navigation-grid builder (our core contribution)

A trained splat is just a cloud of coloured ellipsoids in an arbitrary SfM frame: it has no floor, no scale and no notion of what is solid. navgrid.py turns it into a level, metric, walkable map.

  • Floor: RANSAC over the lowest dense layer (not simply the largest plane, which is often a wall or table top), with normals within 25° of the up hint, then refined to the smallest-eigenvalue axis of the inliers. The scene is rotated so the floor normal is +Y and the floor sits at y = 0. If the up hint is 90° off (ceiling-heavy footage), the camera path still recovers the floor.
  • Obstacles: splats between 0.12 m and 1.9 m, weighted by opacity, are binned into 10 cm cells with a density-adaptive threshold. Blobs under 0.03 m² are dropped as floaters.
  • Free-space carving (depthcarve.py): expected depth is rendered from the training cameras and each ray is marched through 10 cm voxels in the obstacle band. Voxels that rays passed through far more often than they stopped in are floaters and get cleared; ray endpoints add surfaces. Working per voxel matters: a ray passing above a bed says nothing about the bed.
  • Walkability: floor evidence is closed morphologically. Cells the camera physically walked through are always walkable (so doorways stay open), and only floor connected to that path is kept, which removes "rooms" seen through windows and mirrors.
Three independent scale estimates
sm = 0.15 m / ‖ corneri − cornerj ‖
ArUco corners triangulated (DLT) from the COLMAP poses; needs 3+ views with sides agreeing within 5 %
sc = Htape / d(floor, ceiling)
ceiling = second RANSAC above every camera
sh = 1.4 m / median camera height
Priority: marker > ceiling > camera; any estimate > 12 % off is flagged

Web export: PlayCanvas splat-transform removes NaN Gaussians and compresses to SOG (k-means-quantised attributes in lossless WebP, about 10 bytes per splat including all SH bands): a 90 MB PLY became 8 MB. The alignment is stored as a TRS transform in scene.json, so the splat is never resampled, and the grid is plain text that can be fixed in any editor.

5Zero-install web viewer

  • Smooth on ordinary GPUs: Spark builds a level-of-detail tree in a worker, so each frame draws a fixed splat budget whatever the scene size. Splats are clipped at √5σ instead of √8σ to save fill rate, the full-detail cone is widened to 130° so screen edges stay sharp, pixel ratio is capped (lower on phones), and labels are DOM overlays with no extra draw calls.
  • Game-like movement: WASD with exponential acceleration and damping, Shift to run, slight head-bob, F fly mode for a dollhouse view. Movement is resolved per axis, so you slide along walls instead of sticking.
  • Works without a splat: the grid is extruded into a blueprint, so a scene can be checked before training ends.

7Built for Rounds 2 & 3

  • POIs: auto-labelled from a COCO detector (Faster R-CNN), placed in 3D from depth, clustered, kept only when seen from 3+ views; rooms are inferred from contents (a bed makes a bedroom). Press T to tag at the crosshair; labels reveal on click or gaze.
  • Search: ranked fuzzy match (exact > prefix > word-prefix > substring > subsequence) with distances.
  • Routing: 8-connected A* (octile heuristic, no corner cutting) on a grid inflated by a body margin, falling back to the raw grid for tight doors; line-of-sight smoothing; animated floor chevrons; re-plans as you walk.
  • AR (finale): the twin is metric and gravity-aligned, so WebXR needs only one rigid transform from phone tracking to twin (QR anchor at the entrance); the A* route becomes arrows on the real floor.

#  -> localhost:5173 (procedural demo home)
